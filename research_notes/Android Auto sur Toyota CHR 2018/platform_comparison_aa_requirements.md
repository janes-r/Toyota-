# Toyota Touch 2 (CY13/MM16, pre-facelift C-HR) vs Touch 2 "2019 multimedia" (CY17/CY17Plus, MM19): platform differences and Android Auto head-unit requirements

**Labels used on every statement:**
- **[Verified fact]**: stated by an official or primary source (Toyota, Harman, Google, Toyota service bulletins hosted by NHTSA, a project's own README describing that project), or stated the same way by several independent sources.
- **[Informed hypothesis]**: a claim from one community or vendor source, or an inference drawn from documented facts.
- **[Speculation]**: plausible, but no direct evidence was found.

**Method limitation (read first):** In this research session, full-page fetching worked only for github.com. Requests to keenlab.tencent.com, toyota.co.uk, mag.toyota.co.uk, newsroom.toyota.eu, Google support pages, forums (toyotaownersclub.com, chrforums.uk, hybridlife.org, etc.) and milek7.pl all failed with DNS errors. Everything cited from those domains comes from search-engine result excerpts that I could not check against the full page. Quotes from them are reproduced as the search tool returned them. A writer who needs exact wording should re-check those URLs. Scope: feasibility and compatibility only. These notes contain no circumvention, signature-bypass or flashing procedures.

**Owner's unit, for reference:** audio software VE3947, audio device 13TFDAEU-CA04, navigation software 6.11.0WH, navigation device NA530 (from the assignment brief, not independently checked).

## 1. Publicly documented specs of each generation (manufacturer, SoC, RAM, storage, OS, USB, mic, Wi-Fi/BT, video, screen, modules)

### Takeaway
Neither generation has a public hardware datasheet from Toyota or its suppliers. Both use a two-box design: a DCU (Display Control Unit, the "audio" head unit) plus, on "with Go" cars, a separate MEU (Multimedia Extension Unit, the navigation box, made by Harman). The newer EU unit (DCU 17TDT2EU, made by Panasonic, plus MEU 17TMT2EU, made by Harman) is described by one hobbyist project as an NXP i.MX6Q board running Yocto/Automotive Grade Linux on eMMC. No SoC, RAM, storage, OS, screen-resolution or Wi-Fi/BT chipset information could be found for the older 13T-series DCU fitted to the pre-facelift C-HR.

### Inferences
- **[Informed hypothesis]** Under the CY17 naming convention (C = Denso Ten, D = Panasonic), the owner's "-CA04" suffix suggests a Denso Ten (Fujitsu Ten) DCU. The Panasonic 13TFDAEU-DA07 uses a software version in "0101B9" format, while CA04 units report "HE39xx" (forum) or "VE3947" (owner). The different version formats support the idea that CA and DA units run different supplier software stacks. The convention has only been documented for CY17, so this is unconfirmed for 13T units.
- **[Informed hypothesis]** The "13T…" prefix plausibly means calendar-year 2013 platform (CY13), just as "17T…" matches the "CY17" label forums use for the newer unit. If so, the owner's DCU is a 2013 design about four years older than the 17T Panasonic DCU.
- **[Informed hypothesis]** On the pre-facelift C-HR with Go, the documented placement of the USB port on the Harman MEU (mbirth) differs from the newer DCU-hosted CarPlay USB-A port (GitHub project). That would put the phone's USB data connection on a different processor in each generation.
- **[Informed hypothesis]** The two-box DCU+MEU design is a Toyota/Lexus-wide pattern. The Keen Lab NX300 and the EU Touch 2 use the same terms. This says nothing about whether the SoCs are the same.

## 2. Same board with different software, or different hardware generations? Part numbers? Official Toyota statement on hardware limits?

### Takeaway
All available evidence points to different hardware generations, not one board with different software. The DCU model-ID families differ (13T… vs 17TDT2EU), as do the MEU generations (MM13/MM16 vs MM17/MM19). Toyota calls the 2019 unit a "new, 2019 multimedia system", and Toyota's official upgrade programmes cover only 2019+ cars. Forum reports say the facelift unit has physical buttons instead of touch-sensitive ones and needs a wiring harness to fit a 2018 C-HR. No official Toyota statement explicitly says "hardware" is the reason older units cannot get CarPlay/AA.

### Cited Findings
- **[Verified fact]** Toyota Europe describes the CarPlay/AA-capable unit in the facelifted C-HR as "the new, 2019 multimedia system". — [Toyota Europe Newsroom](https://newsroom.toyota.eu/the-new-toyota-c-hr/) (search excerpt)

## 3. What Android Auto needs from a head unit (Google's public material and open-source receivers)

### Takeaway
Google's public help pages say only that the car must be "compatible" (or have an aftermarket AA unit), that a USB port is not enough on its own, and that wireless AA needs 5 GHz Wi-Fi support. The detailed head-unit requirements are in partner-only integration documents. Community reverse-engineering projects describe a TLS-secured link in which the head unit authenticates with an X.509 client certificate, carrying H.264 video plus audio, microphone, input, sensor and Bluetooth channels, over USB (Android Open Accessory) or TCP/Wi-Fi. OpenAuto/aasdk/Crankshaft run on Raspberry Pi-class hardware with a hardware H.264 decoder and openly state they are not certified by Google.

### Cited Findings
- **[Verified fact]** Google's help pages say the car must be compatible with Android Auto or have an aftermarket unit installed, that Android Auto "won't work on all cars equipped with a USB port", and that owners should contact their car manufacturer for model-specific answers. Google also recommends a high-quality USB cable. — [Android Auto Help 6348019](https://support.google.com/androidauto/answer/6348019?hl=en); [Android Auto Help 6348190](https://support.google.com/androidauto/answer/6348190?hl=en) (search excerpts)
- **[Verified fact]** Google lists, for wireless Android Auto, a compatible phone with an active data plan and 5 GHz Wi-Fi support. — [Android Auto Help 6348019](https://support.google.com/androidauto/answer/6348019?hl=en) (search excerpt)
- **[Informed hypothesis — third-party article]** Wireless AA needs both the head unit and the phone to support Wi-Fi Direct on 5 GHz. Many in-car USB ports are charge-only, except the one meant for AA, which is often marked with a phone symbol. — third-party summaries returned alongside Google help: [XDA](https://www.xda-developers.com/android-auto/); [Samsung UK support](https://www.samsung.com/uk/support/mobile-devices/i-am-having-difficulty-connecting-to-or-using-android-auto/) (search excerpt; exact sentence attribution uncertain)
- **[Informed hypothesis — vendor claims]** Commercial test labs say head units need Google's Android Auto certification. They describe requirements covering minimum screen resolution, messaging protocol standards and driver-distraction rules. — [Blue Asia Labs "What is Android Auto Official Certification?"](https://blueasialabs.com/shouyehuandeng/what-is-android-auto-official-certification-); [Blue Asia Labs workflow](https://blueasialabs.com/shouyehuandeng/step-by-step-full-workflow-for-google-android-auto-aa-certification)
- **[Informed hypothesis]** A copy of a Google "Head Unit Integration Guide" (v1.3) is hosted on a third-party site. That a partner integration guide exists is consistent with AA head-unit integration being licensed to OEMs/suppliers. The page could not be fetched, so its contents are not used here. — [milek7.pl huig13_cache](https://milek7.pl/.stuff/galdocs/huig13_cache.html)
- **[Informed hypothesis — open-source reimplementation docs]** The `android-auto` Rust crate describes the wire format as binary framing over a TLS-secured transport. The phone is the TLS server and the head unit the client, and the head unit authenticates with an X.509 client certificate. Logical channels (video, audio, input, sensors, …) are multiplexed and protobuf-encoded. — [docs.rs android-auto README](https://docs.rs/crate/android-auto/0.3.3/source/README.md); [docs.rs android-auto lib.rs](https://docs.rs/android-auto/0.3.7/src/android_auto/lib.rs.html)
- **[Informed hypothesis — open-source project wiki]** The AARE project (phone-side reimplementation) lists "H.264 hardware encoding of a private virtual display" for video. It reports its testing stops at the certificate check against Google's Desktop Head Unit, which makes certificate validation the main barrier for third-party implementations. — [GitHub prototux/AARE wiki](https://github.com/prototux/AARE/wiki)
- **[Verified fact — project README]** aasdk is a "Library to build AndroidAuto headunit emulator". It implements AOAP (Android Open Accessory Protocol) over USB with hotplug, a TCP transport, "SSL encryption" and the AA protocol. Channels: media audio, system audio, speech audio, audio input (microphone), video, Bluetooth, sensor, control, input. Dependencies: Boost, libusb, OpenSSL, Protocol Buffers. Licence GPLv3. — [GitHub f1xpl/aasdk](https://github.com/f1xpl/aasdk)
- **[Verified fact — project README]** OpenAuto is an "AndroidAuto headunit emulator based on aasdk library and Qt libraries", aimed at the Raspberry Pi 3 (also Linux and Windows). It supports 480p/720p/1080p at 30/60 FPS, with hardware-accelerated decoding on the Pi 3, a wireless mode, touchscreen and button input, and microphone input. Its README states "This software is not certified by Google Inc." — [GitHub f1xpl/openauto](https://github.com/f1xpl/openauto)
- **[Verified fact — project README]** Crankshaft is "A turnkey GNU/Linux solution that transforms a Raspberry Pi to an Android Auto head unit" (GPL-3.0). Its repo topics name openauto, aasdk, rpi3 and rpi4. — [GitHub opencardev/crankshaft](https://github.com/opencardev/crankshaft)

### Inferences
- **[Informed hypothesis]** At a high level, an AA head unit needs:
  - a USB host/accessory-capable data port wired to the processor running the AA stack (or 5 GHz Wi-Fi Direct for wireless);
  - real-time H.264 decode at the projected resolution;
  - a touch input path into that stack;
  - a microphone and audio-output path for speech;
  - Bluetooth HFP for calls;
  - an AA protocol implementation with a certificate the phone accepts, which in practice is issued within Google's partner/certification programme.
- **[Informed hypothesis]** A certified OEM unit's certificate and its AA implementation belong to Toyota/its supplier for specific certified hardware. Open-source receivers state they are not Google-certified, so a legitimate AA port to the old Touch 2 would depend on Toyota/supplier licensing as well as engineering.
- **[Speculation]** An i.MX6-class or R-Car Gen2-class SoC is comparable in capability to the Raspberry Pi 3 that OpenAuto targets, so raw compute is probably not the main barrier. The older DCU's SoC is unknown, though, so this cannot be confirmed.

### Gaps
- Google's official, current head-unit requirements (codec profiles, resolutions, USB speed/current, certification process, certificate issuance) are partner-only. No public Google page with them was found or fetchable.
- No public statement was found on whether AA certification/certificates are issued per manufacturer, per supplier or per head-unit model.

## 4. How Toyota head-unit software updates are distributed and whether they are tied to hardware/part numbers

### Takeaway
Toyota distributes Touch 2 map updates through MyToyota / the e-Store and dealers as USB packages, activated with the unit's Device ID plus a Request Code. DCU and MEU firmware are released per model ID (.kwi files and dealer service bulletins). Official Toyota documents tie map activation to the Device ID. Owner reports say firmware packages are matched to the exact DCU/MEU model ID. No official Toyota document found says whether firmware is cryptographically bound to the hardware.

### Cited Findings
- **[Verified fact]** Toyota Europe's 16MM "Touch 2 with Go (Low/High)" map-update dealer guide says: "The USB stick needs to be FAT 32 formatted, with a capacity of 8 GB or greater. The software should be downloaded to the 'root' file structure of the USB stick". Activation needs the Device ID and a Request Code. — [Toyota Europe MapUpdate_16MM_DealerGuide_English.pdf](http://mapupdatecontent.toyota-europe.com/Documents/Dealer%20Guide/MapUpdate_16MM_DealerGuide_English.pdf) (search excerpt)
- **[Verified fact]** Toyota UK tells Touch 2 with Go owners to get map updates through the e-Store via MyToyota on a USB stick. The Device ID is shown under Setup > General > System Information, and owners should contact Toyota UK multimedia support if it is missing. — [Toyota UK blog, "Toyota Touch 2: How to update your maps and software"](https://blog.toyota.co.uk/toyota-touch-2-how-to-update-your-maps-and-software); [Toyota UK Map Updates](https://www.toyota.co.uk/owners/connectivity/map-updates) (search excerpts)

Toyota/Lexus service material for later connected units requires VIN synchronisation after a head-unit replacement and warns that swapping units between cars can get them blacklisted. ## 6. Overall verdict: is running the newer firmware on the older hardware realistic?

### Takeaway
Based on documented facts, no. The newer firmware targets a different hardware generation: a different DCU model family (17TDT2EU vs 13T…), probably a different supplier (Panasonic "D" vs the owner's "C"-suffix unit), a different navigation module generation (MM19 vs MM16), a different front panel and harness, and the CarPlay/AA USB link hosted on the DCU. Toyota distributes firmware per model ID and never offered CarPlay/AA for CY13/MM16 units. Android Auto also needs a certified implementation and certificate that belong to Toyota/its supplier for specific hardware. A "port" would effectively mean writing a new AA head-unit stack for undocumented hardware without legitimate certification. The only routes owners report working are replacing hardware (a 2019+ OEM unit with harness, an aftermarket AA unit, or an interface module).

### Cited Findings
- **[Verified fact]** Toyota markets the CarPlay/AA-capable unit as a "new, 2019 multimedia system". — [Toyota Europe Newsroom](https://newsroom.toyota.eu/the-new-toyota-c-hr/)
- **[Verified fact]** Official CarPlay/AA upgrade programmes covered only 2019+ models (UK: MY19 Corolla, MY19/20 RAV4; Australia: RAV4/HiAce free, Camry/Corolla hatch/Prius A$199). — [Toyota UK Magazine](https://mag.toyota.co.uk/toyota-apple-carplay-and-android-auto-upgrade/); [Toyota Australia](https://www.toyota.com.au/discover/news/toyota-introduces-apple-carplay-android-auto-on-select-vehicles)
- **[Verified fact]** Toyota firmware and map packages are identified per model ID / Device ID. — [Toyota TSB T-SB-0066-20](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2020/MC-10182969-9999.pdf); [Toyota Europe 16MM dealer guide](http://mapupdatecontent.toyota-europe.com/Documents/Dealer%20Guide/MapUpdate_16MM_DealerGuide_English.pdf)
- **[Verified fact]** Open-source AA receivers are not Google-certified. — [GitHub f1xpl/openauto](https://github.com/f1xpl/openauto)
- **[Informed hypothesis — forum]** The working route reported by owners is a hardware swap: a 2019+ C-HR unit with an adapter harness. — [chrforums.uk thread 5141](https://www.chrforums.uk/threads/head-unit-2017-to-2019-with-carplay-android-auto.5141/)
- **[Informed hypothesis — forum]** The 13TXDAEU-CA04 "late touch 2 gen 2" is described as not supporting AA or CarPlay. — [Toyota Owners Club p.10](https://www.toyotaownersclub.com/forums/topic/189278-firmware-upgrade/page/10/)

### Inferences
- **[Informed hypothesis]** Running the 17TDT2EU firmware on a 13T-series DCU is not conceptually feasible as a firmware swap. Different boards and, probably, suppliers mean different bootloaders, kernels, device drivers and peripheral maps. The old DCU's SoC is not even publicly known, so there is no documented basis for compatibility.
- **[Informed hypothesis]** Even if compute were adequate, adding AA to the old unit would need: a USB data path to the AA-capable processor (on Go-equipped cars this goes to the MEU per one source), H.264 decode/compositing, touch and microphone paths, and a certified AA implementation. All of this is unpublished OEM engineering, and the certification/licensing belongs to Toyota/its suppliers.
- **[Informed hypothesis]** Classification: blocked by hardware differences and licensing/certification; would require significant unpublished engineering; not realistic for an owner.
- **[Speculation]** Toyota/suppliers probably never certified the CY13 platform for AA because its software stack predates AA's 2015 launch and the platform was replaced by CY17. This is not documented.

### Gaps
- No teardown or official spec of the owner's exact 13TFDAEU-CA04 DCU or NA530 navigation unit was found, so the hardware comparison relies on model-ID, supplier and generation evidence rather than component lists.
- No official Toyota statement was found that explicitly cites hardware as the reason.
- Keen Lab, Toyota UK, Toyota Europe newsroom, Google Help and forum pages could not be fetched in full in this session. Their content here comes from search excerpts and should be re-checked before quoting.
